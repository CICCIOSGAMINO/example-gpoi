A good staring point to understand what the European Union is, is starting to understand the landmark treaties that each added a new layer of integration, gradually transforming a coal and steel agreement into a political and economic union of 27 member states.

# Treaties

## The Founding Treaties
After the of WW2, leaders of european countries wanted to ensure that such a devastating would never happen again. They aim to create a common market and a customs union, which would make it economically unfeasible for countries to go to war with each other. The first step was the creation of the European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC) in 1951, which was established by the Treaty of Paris.

- 1951 - Treaty of Paris: Created the European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC) among six founding members: France, West Germany, Italy, Belgium, Netherlands and Luxembourg. It placed strategic industries under a common High Authority, the first supranational institution.

- 1957 - Treaties of Rome: Established the European Economic Community (EEC) for a common market and the European Atomic Energy Community (Euratom) for cooperation in nuclear energy. The EEC aimed to create a customs union and a common market among the six founding members, these treaties set the stage for free movement of goods, people, services and capital.

## Consolidation and Expansion Treaties

- 1973 - First Enlargement: The UK, Ireland and Denmark joined the EEC, marking the first expansion beyond the six founding members. Subsequent enlargements included Greece (1981), Spain/Portugal (1986).

- 1986 - Single European Act: Revamped the ECC's decision-making, set the goal to completing the single market by 1992, and introduced European Political Cooperation (EPC) a precursor to the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP).

- 1992 - Maastricht Treaty: Formally established the European Union, introduced the three-pillar structure (European Communities, Common Foreign and Security Policy, and Justice and Home Affairs), and set the stage for the euro as EU currency.

## Deepening and Institutional Reform Treaties

- 1997 - Amsterdam Treaty: Focused on freedom, security and justice, enhanced the powers of the European Parliament, and introduced the concept of "closer cooperation" among member states.

- 1999/2002 - Introducing the Euro: The euro was introduced as an accounting currency in 1999 and as physical coins and banknotes in 2002 in 12 of the then 15 member states, creating a monetary union among those countries.

- 2001 - Nice Treaty: Reformed the institutional structure to prepare for eastward expansion, including changes to the composition of the European Commission and the voting system in the Council.

- 2004 - Big Enlargement: Ten new member states joined the EU, including Cyprus, Czech Republic, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, Poland, Slovakia and Slovenia.

- 2007 - Lisbon Treaty: Abolished the three-pillar structure, created a permanent President of the European Council, established the High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, and enhanced the powers of the European Parliament.

- 2012 - Fiscal Compact Treaty: An intergovernmental treaty aimed at strengthening fiscal discipline within the EU, particularly among eurozone countries, by requiring balanced budgets and limiting government debt.

## In / Out

- 2013 - Croatia joins the EU, becoming the 28th member state.

- 2016 - Brexit Referendum: The United Kingdom voted to leave the EU, leading to a complex and protracted process of withdrawal that culminated in the UK's official exit on January 31, 2020.

## Tech and Digital Treaties
The EU has cemented its role as a global standard-setter in digital policy. Following landmark laws like the Digital Services Act and the AI Act, the upcoming Digital Fairness Act (2026) represents the next frontier, focusing on consumer protection in the digital environment—targeting addictive design and manipulative "dark patterns"

- 2018 - General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR): A comprehensive data protection law that harmonizes data privacy regulations across the EU, giving individuals greater control over their personal data and imposing strict rules on organizations that handle such data.

- 2024 - Artificial Intelligence Act: A proposed regulation that aims to establish a legal framework for the development and use of artificial intelligence in the EU, focusing on ensuring safety, transparency, and accountability.

- 2024 - Digital Services Act (DSA) and Digital Markets Act (DMA): Proposed regulations that aim to create a safer digital space and establish a level playing field for digital services in the EU, addressing issues such as online content moderation, platform accountability, and competition in digital markets.

- 2024 - EU Cybersecurity Act: A regulation that establishes a framework for cybersecurity certification of products and services in the EU, aiming to enhance the security of digital products and services and increase consumer trust.

## Latest Treaties

- 2025 - European Climate Law: A regulation that sets legally binding targets for reducing greenhouse gas emissions in the EU, aiming to achieve climate neutrality by 2050 and to ensure that all member states contribute to the EU's climate goals.

- 2026 - EU-Mercosur Trade Agreement: A trade agreement between the EU and the Mercosur bloc (Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay) that aims to promote trade and investment between the two regions while addressing issues such as sustainable development and labor rights.

- 2026 - Binding 2040 Climate Target: A regulation that sets a legally binding target for the EU to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2040 compared to 1990 levels, as part of the EU's commitment to achieving climate neutrality by 2050.

# Institutions
The EU has several key institutions that play different roles in the governance and decision-making processes of the union. The main institutions include:

- European Commission
- European Parliament
- Council of the European Union
- European Council

# European Commission
The executive branch of the EU responsible for proposing legislation, implementing decisions, and managing the day-to-day affairs of the union. It is composed of Commissioners from each member state, who are appointed for a five-year term.

(Actually is Ursula von der Leyen, ex Minister of Defence of Germany). The Commission has the exclusive right to initiate legislation and is responsible for ensuring that EU laws are properly implemented and enforced across member states. It also represents the EU in international negotiations and manages the EU budget.

# European Parliament
The directly elected legislative body of the EU that represents the citizens of the member states. It shares legislative power with the Council of the European Union and has the authority to approve or reject legislation proposed by the Commission. Members of the European Parliament (MEPs) are elected every five years by citizens of the member states. The Parliament also has the power to approve the EU budget and to oversee the work of the Commission through hearings and inquiries. The President of the European Parliament is elected by MEPs for a renewable term of two and a half years.

(Actually is Roberta Metsola, ex MEP from Malta). The Parliament is organized into political groups based on ideological affiliations rather than national lines, and it plays a crucial role in shaping EU policies and legislation. The Parliament's powers have been gradually increased through successive treaties, making it a key player in the EU's legislative process and a significant force in representing the interests of EU citizens. So for example, the Parliament has a significant influence on the EU's budgetary decisions and can also approve or reject international agreements negotiated by the Commission.

The European Council meets at least four times a year and is responsible for making high-level decisions on issues such as foreign policy, economic policy, and institutional reforms. # Council of the European Union
lso known as the Council of Ministers, it represents the governments of the member states and shares legislative power with the European Parliament. It is composed of ministers from each member state, who meet in different configurations depending on the policy area being discussed. The Council is responsible for adopting legislation, coordinating policies, and making decisions on issues such as foreign policy and budgetary matters. The presidency of the Council rotates among the member states every six months.

The Council of the European Union, on the other hand, is responsible for adopting legislation and coordinating policies among member states, working closely with the European Parliament to shape EU laws and policies. The Council of the European Union plays a crucial role in the legislative process, as it must approve legislation proposed by the European Commission and agreed upon by the European Parliament for it to become law. The Council also has the authority to adopt decisions on foreign policy and security matters, as well as to coordinate economic policies among member states.


# Glossary

**EU**: European Union, a political and economic union of 27 member states located primarily in Europe.

**EEA**: European Economic Area, an area that provides for the free movement of persons, goods, services, and capital within the European Single Market, which includes the EU member states and three of the four EFTA member states (Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway).

**EU law**: The body of laws and regulations that govern the European Union and its member states.

**EU regulation**: Is a binding legislative act of the European Union that becomes immediately enforceable as law in all member states simultaneously. It must be applied in its entirety across the EU without the need for national governments to pass any enabling legislation.

[Artificial Intelligence Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)

**EU directive**: It a legislative act that set out a goal that EU countries must achive. However, it is up to the individual countries to devise their own laws on how to reach these goals.

[EU single-use plastics directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32019L0904&qid=1664779252884)

**EU decision**: A "decision" is a binding legislative act that is applicable to specific individuals, companies, or member states. It is directly applicable and does not require national implementation.

[EU decision on Croatia adoption to the Euro](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32022D1211&qid=1664779252884)

**EU recommendation**: A non-binding legislative act that allows the institutions to make their views known and to suggest a line of action without imposing any legal obligation on those to whom it is addressed.

[EU recommendation media sector](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32022H1634&qid=1664779252884)

**EU Opinion**: An "opinion" is an instrument that allows the institutions to make a statement in a non-binding fashion, in other words without imposing any legal obligation on those to whom it is addressed. An opinion is not binding. It can be issued by the main EU institutions (Commission, Council, Parliament), the Committee of the Regions and the European Economic and Social Committee. While laws are being made, the committees give opinions from their specific regional or economic and social viewpoint.

[EU opinion on enhancing swift delivery](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A52021IE3668&qid=1664779252884)

**Data protection**: The process of safeguarding important information from corruption, compromise, or loss. In the context of GDPR, it refers to the protection of personal data of individuals.

**Privacy**: The right of individuals to keep their personal information secure and to control how it is collected, used, and shared.

**Personal data**: Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (data subject). This can include names, identification numbers, location data, online identifiers, or factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person.
