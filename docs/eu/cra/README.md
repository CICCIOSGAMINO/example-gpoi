CRA - Cyber Resilience Act
==========================
[TOC]

The CRA is the first-ever EU-wide legislation on cybersecurity, establishing mandatory requirements for products with digital elements (any conneted hardware or software sold in EU) to ensure they are secure throughout their lifecycle. 

It entered into force in December 2024 and will apply in stages starting from 2025, with full application by 2027. The CRA aims to enhance the security of connected products, protect consumers, and foster trust in the digital market. It includes provisions for risk management, vulnerability handling, and incident reporting, as well as obligations for manufacturers, importers, and distributors.

# Key obligations for manufacturers
**Security by design**: Products must be developed with cybersecurity built-in, including risk assessments and secure default configurations .

**Vulnerability handling**: Manufacturers must provide security updates throughout a declared support period (minimum 5 years) .

**Reporting**: Actively exploited vulnerabilities must be reported within 24 hours (early warning), 72 hours (detailed notification), and 14 days (final report) via ENISA's single reporting platform .

**Documentation**: Technical documentation and a Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) must be maintained .

# Scope and classification
Products are classified into default, important, and critical categories, with higher-risk products requiring third-party conformity assessments . Most standard products can be self-assessed by manufacturers .

# Exclusions
Medical devices, motor vehicles, aviation products, and non-commercial open-source software are excluded .

# Enforcement
Non-compliance can result in fines up to €15 million or 2.5% of global annual turnover . Market surveillance authorities (such as Germany's BSI) will enforce the rules
