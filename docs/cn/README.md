China
======
[TOC]

Unlike the EU’s treaty‑based integration, China’s digital legal framework has emerged from a combination of top‑down national strategies, rapid industrial growth, and reactive legislation. Initially focused on economic development and internet infrastructure (e.g., the 1990s “Golden Projects”), China began shifting toward explicit cybersecurity and data governance after the 2013 Snowden revelations and the rise of its domestic tech giants. The 2017 Cybersecurity Law marked the first comprehensive pillar, followed by a wave of foundational laws (Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law) in 2021. In parallel, strategic plans such as the New Generation AI Development Plan (2017) and the 14th Five‑Year Plan for the Digital Economy (2021) set long‑term visions. Today, China is moving from reactive to proactive regulation—drafting dedicated AI legislation, refining cross‑border data rules, and testing data property rights—while maintaining strict state control over content and security.


# Key Laws and Regulations

## Foundational Cybersecurity and Data Laws

- **2017 – Cybersecurity Law (CSL):** First comprehensive law regulating network security, data protection, and critical information infrastructure. Introduced data localization for certain personal information, cybersecurity review, and real‑name registration for online services.

- **2021 – Data Security Law (DSL):** Established a tiered data classification system (regular, important, state secret), imposed security obligations on data processors, and created a national data security coordination mechanism. Regulates cross‑border transfers of important data.

- **2021 – Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL):** China’s GDPR equivalent. Grants individuals rights (access, correction, deletion, portability), requires separate consent for sensitive information, and mandates security assessments for cross‑border transfers of personal data.

- **2022 – Anti‑Telecommunications and Online Fraud Law:** Requires telecom and internet service providers to verify user identities, monitor suspicious activities, and cooperate with law enforcement to prevent online fraud.

## Artificial Intelligence Regulations

- **2017 – New Generation Artificial Intelligence Development Plan (AIDP):** A three‑stage strategic plan to make China the world leader in AI by 2030. Focuses on R&D, talent, ethics, and industrial applications.

- **2023 – Interim Measures for Generative AI Services:** First administrative rules targeting generative AI (e.g., chatbots, image generators). Mandate content legality, respect for intellectual property, labeling of AI‑generated content, and security assessments before public release.

- **2024 – Final Regulation on Generative AI Services:** Replaced interim measures, adding requirements for training data legality, model transparency, periodic security audits, and mechanisms for user complaints.

- **2026 – Artificial Intelligence Law (expected):** Comprehensive law covering AI research, deployment, liability, ethics, and alignment with the 2030 AIDP goals.

## Strategic Digital Economy Frameworks

- **2016 – National Informatization Development Strategy:** Sets goals for becoming a global digital technology leader by 2020, with focus on 5G, cloud computing, big data, IoT.

- **2020 – Data as a Production Factor policy:** Recognizes data as an economic resource, leading to pilot data exchanges and trials for data ownership and trading rights.

- **2021 – 14th Five‑Year Plan for Digital Economy Development:** Targets digital economy to reach 10% of GDP by 2025. Emphasizes digital industrialization, industry digitization, and the “Digital Silk Road”.

- **2023 – Digital China Construction Overall Layout Plan:** Roadmap for “Digital China”, integrating digital government, digital society, and infrastructure (national computing hubs, 6G research, industrial internet).

## Cross‑Border Data and International Agreements

- **2025 – Revised Cross‑Border Data Flow Security Assessment Guidelines:** Eased restrictions for non‑sensitive data but retained strict controls for “important data” and large‑scale personal information.

- **RCEP (2022) – E‑commerce chapter:** Promotes paperless trade and electronic signatures but does not mandate free cross‑border data flows, allowing China’s data localization policies.

- **Digital Silk Road (under BRI):** Collection of MoUs and investment deals to deploy digital infrastructure (5G, fibre optics, smart cities) across Asia, Africa, Europe.

## Recent and Upcoming Legislation

- **2024 – Regulation on Algorithmic Recommendation Services:** Requires transparency in algorithmic decision‑making, user opt‑out options, and labelling of algorithmic content.

- **2026 – Data Rights Law (under discussion):** Aims to legally define ownership, usufruct, and trading rules for data as a production factor.

- **2026 – Cybersecurity Review Measures (revised):** Expands scope to include cloud services and AI platforms, with mandatory reviews for products and services used in critical information infrastructure.

# Institutions

- **Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC):** Primary regulator for internet content, cybersecurity, data protection, and AI. Drafts and enforces CSL, DSL, PIPL. Conducts cross‑border data security assessments, cybersecurity reviews, and operates the “Great Firewall” (content filtering). Implements real‑name registration policy.

- **Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT):** Manages telecommunications, internet infrastructure, software, and electronics industry. Issues licenses for 5G, IoT, cloud services, sets technical standards (including for AI and 6G), and promotes industrial digitisation through “Broadband China” and “New Infrastructure” initiatives.

- **National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC):** Macroeconomic planning agency that designs five‑year plans and long‑term strategies for digital economy. Coordinates investments in “new infrastructure” (data centres, AI computing platforms, industrial internet). Approves major digital projects and negotiates international digital trade agreements.

- **Ministry of Public Security (MPS):** Responsible for cybercrime investigation, network surveillance, and enforcement of cybersecurity laws. Operates CNCERT (National Computer Network Emergency Response Technical Team) and collaborates with CAC on content removal and anti‑fraud campaigns.

- **National People’s Congress (NPC) – Standing Committee:** Supreme legislative body. Adopts and amends digital laws (PIPL, DSL, future AI Law). Reviews national digital development budgets and strategic plans.


# BRICS Cooperation on Digital Technologies
BRICS countries (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa) have increasingly collaborated on digital governance, cybersecurity, and AI development. Key initiatives include:

## Digital Governance Frameworks
BRICS Digital Economy Partnership Framework (2022) and the Data Economy Governance Understanding (2025) promote data sovereignty, cross‑border data flows under national laws, and a fair data economy.

## Artificial Intelligence (AI)
BRICS calls for UN‑led inclusive AI governance. The 2025 Leaders' Statement and AI Outlook Report support joint development of AI models, mutual recognition of standards, and preventing dominance by a few tech superpowers.

## Cybersecurity & Data Protection
BRICS coordinates ICT security through dedicated working groups, promotes joint incident response mechanisms, and aims to reduce dependence on foreign technologies while combating cybercrime.



# Glossary

- **CSL:** Cybersecurity Law (2017) – foundational law on network security, data localization, and critical information infrastructure.

- **PIPL:** Personal Information Protection Law (2021) – China’s GDPR, granting data subject rights and regulating cross‑border transfers.

- **DSL:** Data Security Law (2021) – establishes data classification, security obligations, and national data security coordination.

- **AIDP:** New Generation Artificial Intelligence Development Plan (2017) – strategic roadmap to make China world AI leader by 2030.

- **Generative AI Measures:** Interim (2023) and final (2024) administrative rules regulating generative AI services (content labelling, security assessments, training data legality).

- **Important Data:** Legal term under DSL – data whose leakage may harm national security, public interests, or citizens’ rights; subject to cross‑border security assessments.

- **Digital Silk Road (DSR):** Digital component of Belt and Road Initiative – cross‑border digital infrastructure projects and technology standards export.

- **New Infrastructure (xin jichu sheshi):** Policy term covering digital infrastructure: 5G, data centres, AI computing, industrial internet, IoT.

- **Real‑Name Registration:** CAC‑enforced policy requiring online service users to register with government‑issued ID or mobile phone number.

- **Great Firewall (GFW):** System of technical and administrative measures (blocking, filtering) operated by CAC and MPS to control internet content.

- **Cross‑Border Data Transfer Security Assessment:** Mandatory review by CAC for transferring “important data” or large volumes of personal information outside China.

- **Data as a Production Factor:** 2020 policy concept treating data as economic resource, leading to pilot data exchanges and proposed Data Rights Law.

- **RCEP:** Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (2022) – includes e‑commerce chapter allowing China’s data localization policies.

- **CNCERT:** National Computer Network Emergency Response Technical Team – operated by MPS for cybersecurity incident response.

- **Cybersecurity Review:** Mandatory process under CSL for products and services used in critical information infrastructure, expanded in 2026 to include AI platforms.
